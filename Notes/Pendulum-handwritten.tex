The equation for a simple harmonic oscillator

\begin{equation}
\frac{d^2 q}{d t^2}+\omega^2 q=0
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
& {\left[\begin{array}{l}
d^{\prime} \\
v^{\prime}
\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}{ll}
0 & -1 \\
\omega^2 & 0
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{l}
d \\
v
\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{l}
0 \\
0
\end{array}\right]} \\
& \text { or: } \\
& u^{\prime}=L u \text { where } u=\left[\begin{array}{l}
d \\
v
\end{array}\right], L=\left[\begin{array}{cc}
0 & 1 \\
-\omega^2 & 0
\end{array}\right]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The finite difference form:

$$
u^{n+1}=P u^n \text {, where } P=\left[\begin{array}{cc}
1 & \Delta t \\
-\Delta t \omega^2 & 1
\end{array}\right]
$$

$P$ is the simplification matrix
$\operatorname{det}(P)=1+\omega^2 \Delta t^2 \rightarrow$ eigenvalues ere

$$
z_1=1+\Delta t \omega_i \quad z_2=1-\Delta t \omega_i
$$

The largest is the spetad radius $f$.
Nooluhes $\rho=\left|z_1\right|=\left|z_2\right|=\sqrt{1+\Delta^2 w^2}$.
